Catenary model

Described by:

$\coord{z}{v} = \frac{\cosh{(\coord{x}{v}~\catC)} - 1}{\catC}, \quad \coord{x}{v} \in \left[-\catD-\catdD,~\catD\right]$

  • Defined in the vertical plane $\lplanefullxz{v}$
  • With $\catC \in \mathbb{R}^*_+$
  • Homogeneous
  • Non elastic
  • Infinitely flexible
  • Only subject to weight

In water, Buoyancy << Weight

Geometric parameters are:

  • $\fvect{a}{v}$ and $\fvect{b}{v}$ the attachment points
  • $\catL$ the length of the cable
  • $\catC$ the catenary parameter
  • $\catH$ the sag
  • $\catdH$ vertical distance between $\vect{a}$ and $\vect{b}$
  • $\catD$ the horizontal half-distance
  • $\catdD$ the horizontal offset
  • $\catl = \catdD + 2\catD$
Knowing $\fvect{a}{v}$ and $\fvect{b}{v}$, we can solve this equation for $\catC$:

$\catC{}^2\left(\catL{}^2 - \catdH{}^2\right) = 4\sinh^2{\left(\frac{\catl{}\catC{}}{2}\right)}$

Augmented catenary model

$\gamma$-augmentation

Define in a plane rotated by $\gamma$ around $\vect{e}$: $\lplane{\gamma}$

Introduced to account for sway motion along $\ldvect{y}{v}$

$\theta$-augmentation

Define in a frame rotated by $\theta$ around $\ldvect{y}{\gamma}$:

$\lframefull{\theta\gamma}$

Introduced to account for sway motion along $\ldvect{x}{v}$

Accuracy metric

$\res{\theta\gamma}(\theta, \gamma) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n} \norm{\fllpoint{p}{w}{m}{k} - \fllpoint{p}{w}{\theta\gamma}{k}(\theta, \gamma)}$

Computation of the augmentation parameters

$(\theta, \gamma) = \argmin_{(\theta, \gamma) \in [-\pi, \pi]^2} \res{\theta\gamma}(\theta, \gamma)$

To asses the maximum potential accuracy of the augmented catenary model

Robot dynamics¹

¹ T. I. Fossen, Handbook of Marine Craft Hydrodynamics and Motion Control. John Wiley & Sons, 2011.

\begin{align} \vect{\dot{\eta}} & = \matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) \vect{\nu} \\ \lrdmatr{M}{rb} \vect{\dot{\nu}} + \lrdmatr{M}{a} \ldvect{\dot{\nu}}{r} + \lrdmatr{C}{rb}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) \vect{\nu} & + \left(\lrdmatr{C}{a}\left(\ldvect{\nu}{r}\right) + \matr{D}\left(\ldvect{\nu}{r}\right)\right) \ldvect{\nu}{r} + \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) = \vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau} \end{align}

  • $\vect{\eta} = \left[\scal{x}, \scal{y}, \scal{z}, \scal{\phi}, \scal{\theta}, \scal{\psi}\right]^{\top}$ is the state in the world frame $\lframe{W}$
  • $\vect{\nu} = \left[\scal{u}, \scal{v}, \scal{w}, \scal{p}, \scal{q}, \scal{r}\right]^{\top}$ is the linear and angular velocity in the body frame $\lframe{B}$
  • $\matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ is the transformation matrix from body to world frame
  • $\ldvect{\nu}{r} = \vect{\nu} - \ldvect{\nu}{w}$ is the relative velocity between the robot and the water
  • $\lrdmatr{M}{rb}$ and $\lrdmatr{M}{a}$ are the rigid-body and added-mass inertia matrices
  • $\lrdmatr{C}{rb}$ and $\lrdmatr{C}{a}$ are the associated Coriolis matrices
  • $\matr{D}$ is the hydrodynamic damping matrix
  • $\vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ represents the hydrostatic restoring force
  • $\vect{\omega}$ accounts for external forces
  • $\vect{\tau}$ is the control input

With irrotational current and negligible Coriolis effects¹:

¹ T. I. Fossen, Handbook of Marine Craft Hydrodynamics and Motion Control. John Wiley & Sons, 2011.

\begin{align} \vect{\dot{\eta}} & = \matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) \vect{\nu} \\ \matr{I} \vect{\dot{\nu}} + \matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) \vect{\nu} &+ \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) = \vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau} \end{align}

  • $\matr{I}$ denotes the generalized inertia matrix
  • $\matr{D}$ denotes the generalized damping matrix

And can be rewritten as:

\begin{align} \begin{bmatrix} \lrdmatr{I}{6\times 6} & 0 \\ 0 & \matr{I} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \vect{\dot{\eta}} \\ \vect{\dot{\nu}} \end{bmatrix} & = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) \\ 0 & - \matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \vect{\eta} \\ \vect{\nu} \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau} - \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) \end{bmatrix} \end{align}

Cable model

Catenary model

Described by:

$\coord{z}{v} = \frac{\cosh{(\coord{x}{v}~\catC)} - 1}{\catC}, \quad \coord{x}{v} \in \left[-\catD-\catdD,~\catD\right]$

Augmentation angles are not used here to focus on vertical cable clearance

Cable model's forces on its endpoints

The forces are described by:

\begin{align} \fldvect{\omega}{v}{a} &= \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\ldscal{\lambda}{m}\scal{g}}{\catC{}}\\ 0 \\ \frac{\ldscal{\lambda}{m}\scal{g}}{\catC{}}\sinh{\left(-\catC\left(\catD+\catdD\right)\right)} \end{bmatrix} \\ \fldvect{\omega}{v}{b} &= \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\ldscal{\lambda}{m}\scal{g}}{\catC{}}\\ 0 \\ \frac{\ldscal{\lambda}{m}\scal{g}}{\catC{}}\sinh{\left(\catC\catD\right)} \end{bmatrix} \end{align}

Where $\ldscal{\lambda}{m}$ is the linear mass of the cable and $\scal{g}$ is the gravitational acceleration

Robot chain model

Definition

Let the state and input vectors of a robot chain of size $n$ be defined as:

\begin{align} \vect{x} &= \left[\ldvect{\eta}{0}, \ldvect{\nu}{0}, \dots, \ldvect{\eta}{n-1}, \ldvect{\nu}{n-1}\right]^{\top} \\ \vect{u} &= \left[\ldvect{\tau}{0}, \dots, \ldvect{\tau}{n-1}\right]^{\top} \end{align}

Reactive control

The visual-servoing inspired¹ control is:

¹ M. L. Moreira, « Visual servoing on deformable objects: an application to tether shape control », PhD Thesis, Université de Toulon, 2019. https://theses.hal.science/tel-02534996

\begin{align} \ldvect{v}{i+1} = - \lambda \lrumatr{L}{\dagger} \left( \ldvect{s}{i+1} - \luvect{s}{\text{target}} \right) \end{align}

With the feature between robots $i$ and $i+1$:

\begin{align} \ldvect{s}{i(i+1)} = \left[\frac{\ldscal{H}{i(i+1)}}{\ldscal{H}{\text{max}}}, \sin{\scal{\alpha}}, \frac{\ldscal{\Delta H}{i(i+1)} + \ldscal{\Delta H}{\text{max}}}{2\ldscal{\Delta H}{\text{max}}}\right] \end{align}

Robot $i+1$ has no consideration for robots other than its leader: robot $i$

Path planning

The optimal path is:

\begin{align} \vect{p} = \argmin{\lures{h}{\top} \matr{P} \res{h} + \frac{1}{\mpch{}}\sum_{k=0}^{\mpch{}-1}{\lures{k}{\top} \matr{Q} \res{k} + o\left(\ldvect{x}{k}, \ldvect{u}{k}\right)}} \end{align}

With:

  • $\vect{p}=\left(\ldvect{x}{0}, \dots,\ldvect{x}{n}\right)$
  • $\res{k} = \ldvect{x}{k}-\llvect{x}{\text{target}}{k}$ the error at step $k$
  • $\matr{P}$ and $\matr{Q}$ weighting matrices
  • $o(\ldvect{x}{k}, \ldvect{u}{k})$ an objective function

Considering constraints on:

  • Cable's distance to the seabed and the surface
  • Inter-robot distance
  • Robot's speed

Path is tracked with a simple controller

Path is re-planned using varrying criteria

Model predictive control

The optimal control is:

\begin{align} \vect{u} = \argmin{\lures{h}{\top} \matr{P} \res{h} + \frac{1}{\mpch{}}\sum_{k=0}^{\mpch{}-1}{\lures{k}{\top} \matr{Q} \res{k} + \Delta \llvect{u}{\top}{k} \matr{R} \Delta \ldvect{u}{k} + o\left(\ldvect{x}{k}, \ldvect{u}{k}\right)}} \end{align}

With:

  • $\vect{u}=\left(\ldvect{u}{0}, \dots, \ldvect{u}{h}\right)$
  • $\res{k} = \ldvect{x}{k}-\llvect{x}{\text{target}}{k}$ the error at step $k$
  • $\Delta \ldvect{u}{k} = \ldvect{u}{k} - \ldvect{u}{k-1}$ the incremental control at step $k$
  • $\matr{P}$, $\matr{Q}$ and $\matr{R}$ weighting matrices
  • $o(\ldvect{x}{k}, \ldvect{u}{k})$ an objective function

Considering constraints on:

  • Cable's distance to the seabed and the surface
  • Inter-robot distance
  • Robot's speed
  • Robot's actuation

$\ldvect{u}{0}$ is used and the problem is solved again
